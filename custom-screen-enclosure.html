Custom Screen Enclosure

Odd lot lines, wraparound pools, and multi-level patios need custom thinking. Chris Westcott scopes non-standard screen enclosures and coordinates partners when engineering must match a one-of-a-kind footprint.

When “standard cage” catalogs fall short

Not every Tampa Bay backyard is a clean rectangle behind a production home. Waterfront setbacks, kidney-shaped pools, existing outdoor kitchens, and elevation changes force custom screen enclosure design. Forcing a catalog bay pattern onto those sites creates ugly splices, poor door placement, or water traps on the roof.

Chris starts with how the property actually works — views you want to keep, trees you cannot remove, and where storms push water. That scoping conversation is Screen Team’s strength. When the design becomes permit-heavy, licensed partners provide engineering while you keep one primary relationship.

Custom elements we plan around

  • Irregular pool shapes and spa offsets
  • Multi-door access to docks, side yards, or kitchens
  • Partial knee walls, glass accents, or mixed elevations
  • Transitions between lanai and pool cage volumes
  • Integrated super gutter drainage on complex roofs
Custom tip: Walk the yard at 5 p.m. and note where you stand. Door locations should protect those view cones, not block them with a post.

Custom does not always mean brand-new structure

Sometimes “custom” is a hybrid: keep a sound main cage, rebuild a failed wing, or redesign only the house connection. Chris will chase the smallest honest scope that solves the problem — including full rescreens when mesh is the real issue. See also enclosure installation and new pool cages.

Field realities that force custom design

Custom screen enclosures appear on kidney pools, pie-shaped lots, and homes where a dock path must stay open. Chris sketches door vectors against those constraints before anyone commits to bay spacing. The goal is fewer awkward posts in your sightline and fewer water traps on complex roof planes.

Sometimes the custom answer is hybrid: keep the main cage, redesign a failed connector, and rescreen what still stands true. That approach can save months versus a full tear-down. When stamped engineering is mandatory, licensed partners enter with a clear brief Screen Team helped you build — not a vague “make it nice” email.

Waterfront Clearwater and Gulfport properties add salt and wind into the brief. Inland Wesley Chapel lots may care more about preserve insects and two-story massing. Tell Chris which pressures matter on your site so the custom plan is local, not generic.
