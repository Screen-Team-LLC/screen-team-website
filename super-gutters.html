What super gutters are — and why cages need them

A super gutter is a larger-capacity gutter system sized for screen enclosure and pool cage roof runoff. Standard five-inch house gutters often cannot keep up with the volume that hits a cage roof in a Florida downpour. Water sheets off the screening, overwhelms undersized troughs, and stains fascias, floods patios, and erodes planting beds along the enclosure line.

Chris treats super gutters as core work alongside screening. If your cage is tight but every storm leaves a waterfall at the house connection, the mesh is not the problem — drainage is. This page is the hub for installation, repair, pool cage gutter replacement, and related seamless gutter conversations.

Signs your enclosure needs super gutter attention

  • Overflow at corners during ordinary summer storms
  • Sagging or pulled-away runs along the cage header
  • Staining on the house wall where the enclosure attaches
  • Standing water on the lanai after rain stops
  • Downspouts that cannot keep up with roof sheet flow
Gutter tip: Watch one storm from inside the cage. If water freefalls in sheets at the house line instead of entering the trough, capacity or pitch is failing — photo it for Chris.

Install, repair, or replace

Some runs only need reseating, hanger replacement, or seam sealing. Others are the wrong size for the cage and should be replaced with a true super gutter profile. Chris quotes the smaller honest fix when it will work, and recommends new super gutter installation when repair would be throwing money at an undersized system. Pairing with a full cage rescreen often saves a second ladder setup.
