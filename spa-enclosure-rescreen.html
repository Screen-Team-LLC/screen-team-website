Spa Enclosure Rescreen

Spa wings and standalone spa enclosures cook in Florida heat and steam. Mesh near spas ages faster, tears at corners, and lets gnats into the soak. Chris Westcott rescreens spa sections — solo or bundled with the main pool cage.

Why spa screens wear out sooner

Heat, humidity, and chemical mist around a spa accelerate mesh oxidation. Corners where the spa wing meets the main cage collect stress when people lean on kick plates climbing in and out. Homeowners often notice spa-side tears months before the far wall of the pool cage shows the same failure — which is why a spa-only rescreen is a common, honest scope.

Chris looks at whether the spa section shares posts with the main cage, how the roof ties in, and whether door access to the spa needs pet or fine mesh. You are not pushed into a full-cage job if the pool walls are still tight.

Spa wing and spa room options

  • Spa wing panel replacement attached to an existing pool cage
  • Standalone spa enclosure rescreens on sound frames
  • Bundle with full cage work when both areas are oxidized
  • No-see-um upgrades where evening insects ruin soaks
  • Door and latch fixes on spa entries
Spa tip: If you see white powdery oxidation densest on the panels closest to the spa, plan those bays first — steam and splash shorten their life relative to distant walls.

Coordinate with the main enclosure

When the spa wing and pool cage are within a season of the same age, bundling avoids a second setup fee later. If only the spa failed, Chris quotes the wing alone. Related services: pool enclosures, door repair, super gutters if water dumps onto the spa roof line.
