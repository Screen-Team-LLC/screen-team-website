No-See-Um Screen Tampa Bay

Standard mesh stops many bugs — not all of them. No-see-um screen uses a finer weave for coastal and evening insect pressure. Chris Westcott installs fine mesh upgrades on Tampa Bay cages, lanais, and doors where tiny biters ruin outdoor time.

When finer mesh is worth the trade-off

No-see-um screening (fine mesh) reduces gaps that let midges and other small insects through ordinary fiberglass. Waterfront Clearwater, Dunedin, Tarpon Springs, and mangrove-adjacent Pasco yards feel the difference first. The trade-offs are real: slightly reduced airflow and a subtler view change. Chris explains those before you commit so the upgrade matches how you use the space.

You can go full-cage fine mesh or limit no-see-um to doors, spa wings, and seating walls where people linger. Targeted upgrades often deliver most of the comfort without changing every roof bay.

Where Chris recommends fine mesh

  • Evening lanai seating near water or preserve land
  • Spa enclosures used after dusk
  • Door leaves that are the main insect entry
  • Full cage rescreens on coastal lots
  • Homes that already tried standard mesh and still complain of tiny insects
Fine-mesh tip: If you only get bitten while sitting by the door, upgrade the leaf and adjacent panels first — full-roof no-see-um can wait.

Pairing with other mesh types

Pet-resistant mesh and no-see-um solve different problems. Dogs need strength; midges need tighter weave. Some doors get pet mesh while seating walls get fine mesh. See pet-resistant screen, rescreens, and lanai screens.

Coastal evenings and preserve edges

No-see-um screen earns its keep where standard weave still lets tiny insects through after dusk. Mangrove edges, Intracoastal lots, and homes backing to preserves see the strongest demand. Chris places fine mesh where people sit and enter first, then expands to full-cage upgrades only when the insect pattern justifies the airflow trade-off.

Expect a slightly denser look through the fabric. Most homeowners adapt within a day; the comfort gain at sunset is the point. If pets are also shredding doors, combine zones: pet mesh on the leaf, no-see-um on seating walls. That hybrid is common on Dunedin and Tarpon Springs jobs.

Fine mesh is priced as an upgrade line item on rescreens so you can compare against standard fiberglass before approving. Bring your city and a note about when bites happen — afternoon versus after dark changes the recommendation.
