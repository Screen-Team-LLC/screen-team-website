What changes during a lanai replacement

  • Mesh type — standard fiberglass, better visibility, or pet-resistant upgrades on doors and lower walls
  • Door rebuild — hinge-side spline and latch alignment included when the entry failed first
  • Roof panels — horizontal sections tensioned to shed afternoon Florida rain without ponding
  • Track inspection — Chris flags bent members or corrosion before new mesh hides the problem
  • Debris removal — old spline and brittle mesh hauled off, not left on the lanai floor

Replacement vs repair vs a full pool cage

Lanai repair handles one or two torn sections when the rest of the enclosure is still white and tight. Replacement makes sense when oxidation is uniform or you want one clean color before listing. If your “lanai” is actually a freestanding pool enclosure with a full roof grid, you may need a full pool cage rescreen instead — different footprint, different ladder plan, different price band. Localized cage tears still land on pool cage repair. Chris prices each path honestly; he does not earn more by pushing a cage-wide invoice when a door panel would suffice.

Water above the lanai — do not ignore the header

Overflowing house or enclosure gutters stain top panels and soften spline until walls fail again after a refresh. If dark streaks sit above your worst bays, pair the replacement conversation with super gutters, super gutter installation, or pool cage gutter replacement. Combined scopes are outlined on gutters and screens. Fixing mesh while water still sheets onto the header is how Tampa Bay homeowners pay twice.

When the structure itself is the problem

Lanai screen replacement assumes the aluminum frame is still square and serviceable. Bent posts, failed footings, or a collapsed patio enclosure after a named storm are past a mesh-only refresh. That is a new pool cage installation inquiry on our partner path when permits and engineering apply.
