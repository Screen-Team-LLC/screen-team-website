Pool Cage Door Repair

Cage doors take daily traffic — pets, groceries, and kids yanking the latch. When the door leaf tears or drags, bugs walk straight into the enclosure. Chris Westcott repairs and rescreens pool cage doors across Tampa Bay without forcing a full-cage upsell.

Door problems Chris sees every week

Pool cage doors fail at predictable points: hinge-side mesh tears from slam cycles, kick-plate corners where dogs push through, spline that pops at the latch stile, and leaves that drag because sagging nearby panels pulled the frame out of square. A door that will not close completely is not a minor annoyance in Florida — it is an open invitation for mosquitoes and lizards.

Chris isolates whether the leaf needs new mesh, the closer needs adjustment, hinges need tightening, or the surrounding wall panels are pulling the opening out of plumb. You get a door-focused quote, with optional add-ons only if they genuinely fix the seal.

Typical door repair scope

  • Door leaf rescreen with matched or upgraded mesh
  • Spline replacement and corner cleanup
  • Latch and strike alignment so the door clicks shut
  • Hinge and closer checks for slam damage
  • Pet-resistant upgrades on high-traffic doors
Door tip: If the leaf closes but light shows along the latch edge, the wall panel beside the door may be slack. Fixing only the leaf can leave the same gap — Chris checks both.

When the door is a symptom of a bigger cage issue

Sometimes the door is fine and the adjacent bay is not. Storm-torn walls next to the entry, oxidized full cages, or a full pool cage rescreen may be the lasting fix. Chris will not hide that conversation. Related pages: screen door repair, pet-resistant mesh, spa wing rescreen.
