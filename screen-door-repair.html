Doors are the weak link — on purpose

Screen doors move every day. Mesh sees hands, pets, and grocery bags. Tracks collect grit. Closers fatigue. That is why door-specific repair is one of the highest-value small jobs Screen Team handles: fix the entry without rebuilding the whole cage.

Chris distinguishes pool cage door repair on hinged leaves from sliding lanai doors that need track and roller attention. Bring photos of the door type — the quote language changes with the hardware.

What door repair can include

  • Mesh and spline replacement on the door leaf
  • Pet-resistant upgrades for dog traffic
  • Latch, strike, and closer adjustments
  • Sliding track cleaning and roller assessment
  • Advice when adjacent wall panels cause the gap
Door tip: If a slider jumps the track only when humidity spikes, grit plus swollen rollers may both be involved — mention seasonal behavior when you call.

Hinged leaves, sliders, and daily traffic

Screen door repair succeeds when the door type is identified correctly. Hinged pool cage doors usually need mesh, spline, and latch work. Lanai sliders add track grit, roller wear, and jump-off problems after storms blow debris into the channel. Chris carries the diagnosis language for both so you are not sold a cage-door fix for a slider problem.

Pet households should consider stronger mesh on the leaf even when walls stay standard fiberglass. That targeted upgrade absorbs claws where they actually land. If the wall beside the door is slack, sealing the leaf alone leaves a light gap — adjacent panel work may be recommended alongside the door.

Small door jobs still observe the visit minimum on our pricing page because travel and setup are real costs. Bundling a door repair with window screens or a gutter look often makes the trip more efficient for both sides. Text both sides of the door and the track if it is a slider.
