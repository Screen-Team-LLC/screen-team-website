Pool Cage Repair — Tampa Bay, FL

Torn panels, loose spline, and doors that drag — Chris Westcott fixes what failed and tells you when a full rescreen makes more sense.

Repair means fixing the failure — not selling a whole cage refresh

Most pool cage repair calls across Tampa Bay are not catastrophic: a dog pushed through the door mesh, a storm shredded the top horizontal row, or oxidation let one wall tear in a straight line. The Screen Team LLC starts every job by checking the aluminum — plumb posts, solid footings, straight headers. If the structure is sound, Chris Westcott replaces only the panels that failed. If the frame is bent or mesh is chalky everywhere, he says that plainly before quoting a full pool cage rescreen. That honesty keeps repair a real service instead of a soft upsell into the largest invoice in the book.

Call or text (727) 386-6562 with wide shots and close-ups of each tear. Many Pasco, Pinellas, and Hillsborough repairs are ballparked from photos before anyone drives out from New Port Richey.

Common pool cage failures we fix

  • Roof horizontals torn — wind lifts the top row; see also storm screen repair
  • Door spline blown out — mesh pops free on the hinge side after years of traffic
  • Sagging lower panels — weight and UV slacken mesh near the pool deck
  • Pet punctures — upgrade paths on pet-resistant mesh
  • Corroded screen beads — Chris reseats spline instead of masking brittle mesh
  • Isolated wall bays — when only one or two sections failed; also screen panel repair
Repair vs rescreen: If three walls look gray and brittle, patching panel four wastes money when panel five fails next month. Chris explains that tradeoff on the phone — no pressure toward a bigger job than you need. When the whole perimeter is spent, the full-cage page is the honest next click.

Drainage problems that masquerade as mesh failure

Undersized enclosure troughs dump water onto headers and soften spline until roof panels tear in the same bay after every cell. If overflow stains sit above your worst panels, ask Chris to inspect super gutters while he is on the ladder. New capacity runs are covered on super gutter installation; wrong-profile troughs belong on pool cage gutter replacement. Combined drip-and-tear scopes live on gutters and screens. Repairing mesh while water still freefalls onto the frame is how homeowners buy the same panel twice.

When repair is not enough — rescreen or new structure

Uniform oxidation, slack roof panels that pond after rain, and doors that no longer seal because surrounding fabric lost tension are classic signs a partial repair will look and last poorly. That is when Chris recommends a coordinated full pool cage rescreen. If posts are bent, footings failed, or the cage collapsed, you are past mesh work — start a new pool cage installation conversation on our partner path when permits and engineering apply. Screen Team does not invent licensing claims; partners handle permit-required builds while Chris remains your owner contact.

How a repair visit runs

Chris confirms which bays failed, pulls worn mesh, checks the track, rolls new screening with spline seated for Florida heat, and aligns the door if the entry was part of the scope. Debris is cleared from the deck. If he finds structural red flags during the walk, he stops and quotes the real path — rescreen, gutter work, or partner-path new build — before spending your money on a temporary patch.
